Privacy Policy

Pulse Insights for Android phones and Wear OS watches · Last updated: 7 November 2026

In short: Pulse has no accounts, no analytics and no ads, and we don't receive, sell or share your data. Your health data is read and kept on your own devices. What leaves them is listed below: your own Android backup, what the map providers receive, and anything you choose to share.

Health data

With your permission, Pulse reads from Health Connect: your workouts and their routes, distance, active and total calories, basal metabolic rate (only to work out active calories where a source writes only totals), heart rate, steps, activity intensity, sleep and VO₂ Max, including data older than 30 days. It uses them only to show you your trends, in the app and in reports you choose to create.

Pulse writes to Health Connect only the workouts you record with the Pulse app on your watch: the session, its route, distance, calories, heart rate, steps and elevation. The watch hands each workout to the Pulse app on your phone, which saves it to Health Connect.

What Pulse reads is kept in a cache on your phone so your dashboards open quickly. It is excluded from backups and deleted with Profile › Delete Account.

The use and transfer of information Pulse receives from Health Connect follows the Health Connect Permissions policy, including its Limited Use requirements: it is used only to provide the features you see, never for advertising, never sold, and never read by people.

Your watch

While you record a workout, the watch reads your heart rate and what it measures of the effort (calories, steps, cadence, running form and a pool's laps) from its own sensors, and on an outdoor workout its location (GPS). The watch doesn't use location at any other time. With your permission it also counts today's steps and distance, for its Today page.

It keeps your recordings on the watch for its own workout history, until you uninstall Pulse there. It hands each workout to your phone to save to Health Connect; the cadence, running-form and lap figures stay on the watch.

Your phone and watch exchange workouts, your interval timers, today's activity figures and your ring goals through Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer. When the two aren't near each other, Google may relay this exchange between them, end-to-end encrypted.

Interval timers

On Android 14 and 15, your phone asks for the physical activity permission when you start an interval timer, because Android keeps a timer running with the screen off only with it. Pulse reads nothing with this permission. If you refuse, the timer still runs, with the screen kept on.

Maps

A workout's map on your phone is shown with the Google Maps SDK, under Google's Privacy Policy. Maps on your watch are downloaded from Mapbox. Each map request tells the provider which area of the map to send, which can reveal roughly where you are, and carries your IP address. No health or profile data goes with it.

When your phone shows a map, the Google Maps SDK also sends Google some technical data of its own: details of your device (such as its model and Android version), crash reports from the SDK, a pseudonymous identifier it uses to count its users, and how you pan and zoom the map. Google says it uses these to run and improve its services. They go straight to Google and never reach us.

Information you enter

Your name, an optional email address for reports, and optionally your height, weight and birth date (used to estimate your heart-rate zones) are stored on your phone. With your ring goals, your settings and your interval timers, they are included in your phone's Android backup, which is kept in your own Google account under its own terms. Your health data is never included.

Reports and sharing

Pulse can create a PDF report or an image of a workout. These are made on your phone and leave it only when you share or email them yourself, through Android's own sharing. Pulse never sends anything automatically: the weekly reminder only opens last week's report for you to send.

Data we collect

None reaches us. Pulse has no accounts, no servers of its own and no analytics, and it contains no advertising or tracking SDKs. The map providers receive what the Maps section above describes.

Deleting your data

Profile › Delete Account removes your profile and everything Pulse has read from Health Connect. You can stop Pulse's access at any time in Health Connect, and uninstalling Pulse from your phone or watch removes everything it stored there. The workouts Pulse saved to Health Connect stay there until you delete them in Health Connect.

Children

Pulse is not designed for children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
