Your data in Pulse

Why does Pulse ask for Health Connect access?

Pulse shows trends from what's in Health Connect: your workouts and their routes, distance, calories, heart rate, steps, sleep and VO₂ Max. It writes there only the workouts you record with Pulse on your watch. You choose what it can read and save, and you can change that at any time in Health Connect, under App permissions › Pulse.

I allowed access but see no data.

Pulse shows what apps and watches have saved to Health Connect, so check that the one recording your activity saves there: many have a setting for it. Then check Pulse's access in Health Connect, under App permissions › Pulse, where “Allow all” turns everything on. Pulse picks up changes when you come back to it.

A total looks too low or reads zero.

Health Connect adds up its totals from the apps on its priority list. In Health Connect, open Data and access › Data sources and priority, and check that the app recording your steps, distance or calories is on the list.

Pulse shows only my recent history.

Health Connect lets an app read back 30 days from when you first gave it access, unless you allow more. To see everything, open App permissions › Pulse › Additional access in Health Connect, and turn on Access past data.

Why does a workout ask before showing its route?

Health Connect asks each time before sharing a route that another app recorded. Tap “Show route recorded by…” on the workout. To stop being asked, open App permissions › Pulse › Additional access in Health Connect, and set Access exercise routes to Always allow.

Your watch

Do I need a watch?

Not to see your data. You need Pulse on a Wear OS watch to record workouts with Pulse. For rings, sleep and VO₂ Max, a watch or fitness app that writes to Health Connect is recommended.

A workout from my watch hasn't reached my phone.

The watch keeps each workout in its own history, then hands it to Pulse on your phone, which saves it to Health Connect. If Pulse isn't allowed to write there, the workout waits and a notification tells you: allow it, then open Pulse on your phone. Otherwise, check that your watch and phone are connected. Opening Pulse on either one picks up anything still waiting.

Timers and reports

Why does a timer ask for physical activity?

On Android 14 and 15, Android keeps a timer running with the screen off only with this permission. Pulse reads nothing with it. If you refuse, the timer still runs, with the screen kept on.

How do the weekly reports work?

Pulse creates each report on your phone, and it leaves only when you share or email it yourself: nothing is sent automatically. Turn on Automated weekly email, and every Monday at 9:00 Pulse reminds you and opens last week's report, ready for you to send.

Privacy and your devices

Is my data private?

Pulse has no accounts, no analytics and no ads, and your health data is read and kept on your own devices. The Privacy Policy lists what leaves them: your own Android backup, which never includes health data, what the map providers receive, and anything you choose to share.

How do I delete my data?

In Pulse, Profile › Delete Account removes your profile and everything Pulse has read from Health Connect. Health Connect itself doesn't change, so workouts Pulse saved there stay until you delete them in Health Connect. Uninstalling Pulse from your phone or watch removes everything it stored there.

Which devices are supported?

Phones, tablets and foldables on Android 9 or later, and watches on Wear OS 4 or later. Health Connect is part of Android 14 and later. On earlier versions it's an app from Google Play, and Pulse tells you if it needs an update.
